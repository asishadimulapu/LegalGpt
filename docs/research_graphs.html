Figure 1. (a) Grouped bar chart showing latency per pipeline stage for a fresh query (cache miss) versus a repeated query (cache hit). Embedding generation (HuggingFace all-MiniLM-L6-v2 on CPU) dominates uncached latency at ~300–400 ms; an MD5-keyed LRU cache of size 1,000 reduces this to <1 ms for repeated queries. Hybrid search (pgvector cosine + PostgreSQL BM25 with Reciprocal Rank Fusion at k=60) adds ≈15–30 ms. LLM generation via Gemini 1.5-Flash or Groq constitutes the dominant end-to-end cost (~800–1,500 ms). (b) Radar comparison of three supported embedding providers across five axes: dimensionality, cost (inverted), local-inference support, retrieval quality (MRR@5 proxy), and cold-start speed. (c) Simulated hit-rate curve showing how cumulative latency savings grow with query volume, reaching ≈79% hit-rate after ~300 distinct legal queries.

Figure 2. (a) Doughnut chart depicting the proportional composition of the LegalGPT knowledge base. The corpus combines curated structured sections (IPC, Constitution, Evidence Act, IT Act) with HuggingFace datasets (viber1/indian-law-dataset, harshitv804/Indian_Penal_Code, Techmaestro369/indian-legal-texts-finetuning) and PDF-parsed statutes. (b) Mean Reciprocal Rank at cutoff 5 (MRR@5) across four representative Indian legal query categories — exact section lookup, general criminal law, constitutional rights, and cyber law — comparing pure vector cosine similarity, BM25 full-text search, and the deployed Reciprocal Rank Fusion hybrid. The hybrid strategy yields consistent gains, particularly for exact section queries (+18% vs. vector-only). (c) Box distribution of per-result cosine similarity scores across IPC section categories, illustrating how the hybrid RRF strategy elevates low-confidence vector matches by incorporating keyword ranking signal.
